Germany: 1890–1945 Textbook Revision

Welcome to the comprehensive textbook revision hub for East Leake Academy GCSE History students. Use the detailed sections below to guide your deep-dive reading and revision notes.

1

Germany and the Kaiser (1890–1914)

Autocracy and Power: Kaiser Wilhelm II believed in the divine right of kings and wanted personal control over policy (Weltpolitik). He dismissed Chancellor Otto von Bismarck in 1890 to steer Germany independently.

Industrialisation & Militarism: Rapid industrial growth saw Germany surpass Britain in iron, steel, and electrical output by 1914. This economic boom fueled an aggressive naval race against Britain via the Naval Laws.

2

The Weimar Republic (1919–1929)

Foundation and Crises: Formed out of the revolution of 1918–1919, the Weimar Republic faced immediate instability. The Treaty of Versailles (1919) crippled morale, while hyperinflation in 1923 and early uprisings tested its democratic framework.

The Golden Years (1924–1929): Under Gustav Stresemann, Germany experienced economic recovery supported by American loans (Dawes Plan), cultural expression, and diplomatic rehabilitation (Locarno Pact).

3

Hitler and the Nazi Dictatorship (1933–1945)

Rise to Power: Capitalising on the Great Depression, Hitler exploited public fear of communism. Following the Reichstag Fire and the Enabling Act (1933), democracy was dismantled.

Control and Terror: Nazi rule relied on a powerful police state (SS, Gestapo), relentless propaganda (Goebbels), and social regimentation (Hitler Youth, Lebensraum ideology).
